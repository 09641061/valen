# Scheduling authorization

Scheduling appearance is denied by default, including for Owner and Administrator. An explicit, configurable `scheduling.appear` grant for the individual is required; neither account creation, group membership, role name, administrative wildcard nor authority to configure Scheduling implies it. The Owner can explicitly enable or revoke their own grant and those of others. Revocation removes eligibility without deleting the account, other permissions or appointment history. A separate active Scheduling state is also required for appearance.

Distinguish permissions to view the calendar, manage appointments, configure Scheduling and appear as a bookable person. Appearance grants no operational authority. Enforce all actions on the backend through the chosen Casbin policy integration; the UI reflects effective capabilities but is not security enforcement. Resolve eligibility per person using an explicit grant that cannot be satisfied by an Owner or Administrator wildcard. Fail closed when authorization is unavailable. Keep IAM the authoritative source, propagate revocations and avoid stale cached decisions.

The grant management mechanism, delegation rules and exact operational permission names remain design decisions. Preserve existing IAM hierarchy and group-management rules; do not assume group membership alone is an appearance grant or that existing group-only grants can represent Owner self-configuration. Test Owner and Administrator without grants, explicit grant and revocation, inactive resources, backend bypass attempts and changes during concurrent booking.
